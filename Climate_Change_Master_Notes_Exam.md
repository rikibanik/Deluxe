## 1. Introduction & Key Definitions

### What is Climate Change?
- Climate change refers to changes in the Earth's climates at **local, regional, or global scales**.
- Most commonly used to describe **anthropogenic (human-caused)** changes.
- Changes may be natural, but **human activities** have been the main driver — primarily due to burning of **fossil fuels** (coal, oil, gas) increasing heat-trapping greenhouse gas levels.

### Key Organizations
| Abbreviation | Full Form |
|---|---|
| **IPCC** | Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change |
| **UNFCCC** | United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change |

### IPCC Definition
> "Climate change is a statistically significant variation in either the mean state of the climate or in its variability, persisting for an extended period. Climate change may be due to natural internal processes or external forcings, or persistent anthropogenic changes in the composition of the atmosphere or in land use." *(Houghton et al., 2001)*

### UNFCCC Definition
> "A change of climate which is attributed directly or indirectly to human activity that alters the composition of the global atmosphere and which is in addition to natural variability observed over comparable time periods." *(UNFCCC, 1994)*

### Key Points
- Climate change is among the most serious environmental problems facing humankind *(UNDP, 2002)*.
- Further exacerbated by failure to address **poverty** and **environmental degradation** *(Norgaard, 1994)*.
- IPCC confirms human activities cause climate change.
- Increased GHG concentrations imply society has moved beyond **"primordial prevention"** *(McMichael & Kovats, 2000)*.
- **Sensitivity and vulnerability** of human and natural systems determine how impacts are experienced.

## 3. The Earth's Climate System

### Climate Change & Energy Balance
- Climate change occurs when the global **energy balance** between incoming energy from the Sun and outgoing heat from Earth is upset.
- The climate system includes: **atmosphere, oceans, ice sheets, living organisms, and rocks** — all affect heat movement around Earth's surface.

### Earth's Climate History
- Earth's climate has changed throughout geologic time.
- Climate has usually been **much warmer** than today.
- Humans evolved in one of the few **cold periods** in Earth's history.
- Today we live in an **interglacial period** during an ice age.

### Components of Climate System (IPCC)
1. **Atmosphere** — N₂, O₂, Ar, H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, O₃, aerosols
2. **Hydrosphere** — oceans, rivers, lakes
3. **Cryosphere** — sea ice, ice sheets, glaciers
4. **Biosphere** — forests, vegetation
5. **Land Surface** — mountains, soil

### Key Drivers of Change
- Changes in solar inputs
- Changes in atmosphere (composition & circulation)
- Changes in hydrological cycle (clouds & precipitation)
- Changes in ocean (circulation, sea level, biogeochemistry)
- Changes on land surface (orography, land use, vegetation, ecosystems)

## 4. Theories of Climate Change

### Common Theory — Anthropogenic Global Warming
- GHGs trap heat (greenhouse effect) keeping Earth at suitable temperature for life.
- Human alteration of GHG concentrations → increased warming (**Global Warming**).
- Mechanism: **Enhanced greenhouse effect** *(Solomon et al., 2007)*.
- Continued fossil fuel burning and deforestation could **double CO₂** in next 100 years if natural sinks don't keep pace *(Bast, 2010)*.

### Other Theories
| # | Theory | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Global Bio-thermostat** | Negative feedbacks from biological/chemical processes offset positive feedbacks (e.g., carbon sequestration, COS) *(Idso & Singer, 2009)* |
| 3 | **Cloud Feedback** | Changes in cloud formation/albedo create negative feedbacks canceling CO₂ warming *(Sud, Walker & Lau, 1999)* |
| 4 | **Land Surface Transformation** | Greatest human influence is transforming Earth's surface — clearing forests, irrigating deserts, building cities *(Pielke Sr, 2009)* |
| 5 | **Thermohaline Circulation** | Temperature variations due to slowdown of ocean's THC *(Gray, 2009)* |
| 6 | **Solar System Oscillations** | Natural gravitational/magnetic oscillations of solar system *(Scafetta & West, 2009)* |
| 7 | **Solar Variability** | Solar variability accounts for most late 20th century warming *(Bast, 2010)* |
| — | **Volcanic eruptions & continental drift** | Also contribute to climate change |

### Factors of Climate Change
1. **Natural Processes**

## 5. Social Dimensions & Vulnerability

### Social Dimensions of Climate Change
- CC threatens **prosperity, growth, equitable sustainable development**, and governance systems *(Mearns & Norton, 2010)*.
- Human systems tied to established climate (e.g., monsoon periodicity); CC creates **societal stress**.
- **Poor people** have fewer resources and rely more on local ecosystems *(Christoplos et al., 2009)*.
- Communities dependent on natural resources are most vulnerable.
- **Indigenous/traditional communities** pushed to fragile territories at greatest risk *(Macchi et al., 2008)*.

### Inequity & Migration
- Glaring inequities in responsibility vs. impacts — poor in developing countries suffer most despite contributing least *(Salick & Byg, 2010)*.
- CC is a major driver of **human migration and displacement** *(Warner, 2011)*.
- CC interacts with other stresses (e.g., drought → rural-urban migration → urban stress) *(Wilbanks et al., 2007)*.
- Marginalized groups adapt in uncoordinated ways; future hazards may exceed adaptive capacity *(Macchi et al., 2008)*.

### Climate Change & Vulnerability (Patnaik & Narayanan, 2009)
| Type | Indicators |
|---|---|
| **Demographic** | Population density, literacy rate |
| **Climatic** | Variance in annual rainfall, JJA rainfall variance, frequency of extreme events |
| **Agricultural** | Rice production, cropping intensity, area under cultivation, irrigation intensity, cattle/livestock |
| **Occupational** | Total workers, agricultural labourers, manufacturing labourers, non-workers |

## 6. Climate Forcing Mechanisms

Mechanisms that upset global energy balance = **"Climate Forcing"**

### 1. Plate Tectonics
- Earth's surface broken into plates; movement ~few cm/year.
- Over millions of years, land area size/position changes.

**Global Cooling:**
- Mountain building → more rock exposed to chemical weathering → CO₂ removed → diminished greenhouse effect.
- Himalaya uplift (India-Asia collision, 50 mya) may have contributed to recent cooling.

**Global Warming:**
- **Flood basalts** release enormous basalt and CO₂ (thousands of cubic miles in few hundred years).
- Largest event 250 mya → ice age to hothouse → largest mass extinction → dinosaur era → petroleum formation.

### 2. Orbital Changes (Milankovitch Cycles)
1. Changes in shape of Earth's orbit
2. Changes in tilt of Earth's axis
3. Wobble of Earth's axis
- Glacial periods need **cooler summers** (not cold winters) for ice accumulation.

### 3. Solar Variability
- Changes inside the Sun affect sunlight intensity → warming or cooling.
- Short-term effect: 10s to 100s of years.
- **Sunspots** = lower temperature areas that actually increase solar radiation.
- More sunspots = more solar radiation.
- Sunspots follow **11-year solar cycle**.
- **Maunder Minimum** (1645–1715): almost no sunspots → **Little Ice Age**.

### 4. Aerosols
- Tiny particles suspended in air; can warm OR cool.
- Natural sources: volcanoes, dust storms, fires.
- Human sources: fossil fuel burning, land use changes.
- **Do NOT cause long-term change** — leave atmosphere quickly.

**Reflective Aerosols (Cooling):**
- Sulfur and nitrogen compounds from biomass/fossil fuel combustion and volcanic eruptions.
- Reflect sunlight → reduce solar radiation reaching surface.
- 1991 Pinatubo eruption → global cooling <1°F for 1–2 years.

**Black Carbon Aerosols (Warming):**
- Soot from incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and wood.
- Absorbs heat; reduces albedo on snow/ice.
- **2nd largest short-term contributor** to warming after GHGs.
- Remains only weeks → reducing BC = fastest near-term mitigation.

### 5. Greenhouse Gases
- Important forcing despite low abundance.
- Natural CO₂ and CH₄ stabilize Earth's temperature.

## 7. Ice Ages & Orbital Changes

### Ice Age Cycles
- Past **450,000 years**: climate fluctuated between warm and cold periods.
- **Glacials (Ice Ages):** ~100,000 years duration.
- **Interglacials (warm intervals):** ~10,000 years duration.
- Polar ice caps grew and shrank with these cycles.
- Most recent glacial period ended ~**11,000 years ago**.

### Rates of Climate Change
| Time Scale | Driver |
|---|---|
| Hundreds of millions of years | Continental drift, flood basalts (plate tectonics) |
| Tens to hundreds of thousands of years | Orbital changes (Milankovitch) |
| Centuries to decades | Solar variability, aerosols, GHG concentrations |
| Hours | Weather changes |

## 8. Greenhouse Gases & Greenhouse Effect

### What are Greenhouse Gases?
Gases that cause the **"Greenhouse Effect"** — trap heat in the atmosphere.

### Contribution to Greenhouse Effect (Approximate)
| Gas | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Water vapour | ~60% |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | ~20% |
| Methane (CH₄), Nitrous oxide (N₂O), Tropospheric ozone (O₃) | ~20% combined |

### GHG Emission Sources (Anthropogenic)
| Source | Share |
|---|---|
| CO₂ from fossil fuel & industrial processes | ~65% |
| CO₂ from forestry & other land use | ~11% |
| Methane | ~16% |
| Nitrous oxide | ~6% |
| F-gases | ~2% |

### Water Vapor — Important Note
- Most efficient at trapping heat; most abundant GHG.
- BUT: stays only **few days** in atmosphere (not a long-term forcing).
- Works as a **feedback mechanism** enhancing climate change, NOT a primary cause of man-made warming.

### Natural GHG Sources
- **CO₂:** volcanic eruptions, respiration, decomposition, fuel combustion.
- **CH₄:** decomposition of organic material, melting methane hydrates, Arctic permafrost.

### Greenhouse Effect — How It Works
1. Sun's **short-wave** solar energy passes through atmosphere.
2. Earth's surface is heated and radiates **long-wave infrared** heat back.
3. GHGs in atmosphere **trap** some of this heat.
4. Some energy reflected back to space.
5. Result: Earth warms (like a car with windows — heat enters but can't escape).

**Car Analogy:** Sunlight passes through windscreen → heats interior → heat can't escape → car becomes hotter.

## 9. Global Warming Potential (GWP) & CO₂ Equivalent

### Global Warming Potential (GWP)
- Each GHG has different GWP and persists different lengths of time.
- Expressed relative to CO₂ over **100 years**.

### Main GHGs — 100-Year GWP (AR4)
| Greenhouse Gas | Formula | 100-Year GWP |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | CO₂ | **1** |
| Methane | CH₄ | **34** |
| Nitrous oxide | N₂O | **298** |
| Sulphur hexafluoride | SF₆ | 22,800 |
| Hydrofluorocarbon-23 | CHF₃ | 14,800 |
| Hydrofluorocarbon-32 | CH₂F₂ | 675 |
| Perfluoromethane | CF₄ | 7,390 |
| Perfluoroethane | C₂F₆ | 12,200 |
| Perfluoropropane | C₃F₈ | 8,830 |
| Perfluorobutane | C₄F₁₀ | 8,860 |
| Perfluorocyclobutane | c-C₄F₈ | 10,300 |
| Perfluoropentane | C₅F₁₂ | 13,300 |
| Perfluorohexane | C₆F₁₄ | 9,300 |

### Key Examples
- **1 kg CH₄** ≈ **34 kg CO₂**
- **1 kg N₂O** ≈ **298 kg CO₂**
- **1 kg SF₆** ≈ **22.8 tonnes CO₂** ≈ driving **5 cars for a year**
- **1 million tonnes CH₄** ≈ **34 million tonnes CO₂**
- **1 million tonnes N₂O** ≈ **298 million tonnes CO₂**

### Carbon Dioxide Equivalent (CO₂e)
- Common unit to compare different GHGs.
- CO₂e = amount of CO₂ with equivalent global warming impact.
- Necessary to convert all GHG emissions to CO₂e for comparison.

## 11. Climate Feedback Mechanisms

### Definition
- **Feedback** = indirect change in climate system in response to a forcing.
- **Positive feedback** = initial change brings additional change in **same direction** (amplifies).
- **Negative feedback** = initial change brings additional change in **opposite direction** (stabilizes).

### Examples in Daily Life
- **Positive:** Interest on savings account — principal grows → more interest → faster growth.
- **Negative:** Body sweating — temperature rises → sweat evaporates → body cools.

> **Note:** Positive (not negative) feedbacks contribute to **abrupt climate changes**.

### Seven Climate Feedback Mechanisms

#### 1. Ocean–Carbon Dioxide Feedback (Positive)
- Cold water holds more CO₂ than warm water.
- Warming → CO₂ evaporates from ocean → more warming → more CO₂ release.
- Cooling → CO₂ dissolves in ocean → more cooling.
- Positive correlation between atmospheric CO₂ and temperature over past 450,000 years.

#### 2. Water Vapor Feedback (Positive)
- Warmer temps → more evaporation → more water vapor (GHG) → warmer temps.
- Warmer air holds more water → more water vapor in atmosphere.

#### 3. Ice-Albedo Feedback (Positive)
- Ice-covered surfaces have **high albedo** → reflect more solar energy.
- Warming → less snow/ice → more solar absorption → more warming.
- Ice age → more ice → more reflection → enhanced cooling.

#### 4. Methane Feedback (Positive)
- CH₄ is **60× more powerful** than CO₂ but lasts only ~10 years (vs CO₂ ~100 years).
- **Methane hydrates** at ocean bottom — more carbon than all oil/gas reserves combined.
- **PETM (55 mya):** Temperatures rose 5–7°C in few thousand years due to methane hydrate release.
- **Arctic permafrost:** Trapped methane from decaying vegetation; melting releases it.

#### 5. Cloud Feedback (Both)
- **Negative:** Low, thick clouds reflect solar radiation → cool surface.
- **Positive:** High, thin clouds transmit solar radiation but trap outgoing infrared → warm surface.

#### 6. Biological Feedback (Negative)
- More vegetation/plankton → removes CO₂ → lowers temperature.
- Terrestrial: photosynthesis + chemical weathering.
- Oceanic: plankton use dissolved CO₂ for shells.

#### 7. Ocean Circulation Feedback
- **Thermohaline circulation:** Driven by temperature (thermal) and salinity (haline) differences.
- North Atlantic: warm salty water from tropics → cools → releases heat → sinks → flows south.
- Warming melts glacial ice → fresh water dilutes salinity → reduces sinking → slows conveyor belt → **cooling**.
- **Younger Dryas (13,000 ya):** Freshwater from melting glaciers disrupted Atlantic circulation → 1,000-year rapid cooling → possible mammoth extinction.

## 12. Abrupt Climate Change

### Definition
- Climate system forced across a **threshold** → transition to new state.
- Rate determined by climate system itself, **faster than the cause**.
- Takes place over **few decades or less**, persists decades+, causes substantial disruptions.
- May occur over region, hemisphere, or entire globe.

### Ball-in-Valley Analogy
- **Weak push:** Ball rolls up hill, rolls back → **climate variability** (returns to stable state).
- **Strong push:** Ball tops hill (unstable state) → rolls into second valley → **abrupt change** to new stable state at higher temperature.

## 13. Global Warming Projections

### Current Trends
- Global temperatures are rising.
- Emissions (million metric tons CO₂e) steadily increasing from 1990–2010+.
- CO₂ is the dominant GHG in emissions.

### Temperature Rise by 2050
- **Global average:** ~2.75°C rise (relative to 1850–1900 baseline).
- **India:** ~2.94°C projected.
- **+2°C before 2050** projected.
- Arctic amplification, geography, weather patterns, ocean currents, and human activities affect regional warming.

### IPCC Confidence at This Warming Level
- Extreme weather events will be **more frequent and intense**.
- Nearly all ecosystems will face **high risks of biodiversity loss**.
- Accelerated sea level rise will threaten **coastal settlements**.

### Key Projections
- Warming and sea level rise will continue, likely **faster** than already seen.
- Even if GHGs stabilized, effects may continue for **centuries**.
- Some effects may be **permanent**.

## 14. Impacts of Climate Change (Global)

### Definition of Impact
- CC leads to changes in **geophysical, biological, and socio-economic systems**.
- **Impact** = specific change in a system caused by exposure to CC (harmful or beneficial) *(Schneider et al., 2007)*.

### Geophysical Changes
- Changes in wind patterns, precipitation, temperature, ocean pH, weather extremes, sea ice, sea level.
- Warming greatest over **land and high northern latitudes**; least over **Southern Ocean**.
- Increased frequency of **heat and precipitation extremes**.
- Increased **tropical cyclone intensity**.
- Increased precipitation in **high latitudes**; decreased in **sub-tropical land regions**.

### Sea Level Rise
- Greenland ice sheet shrinking → rising sea levels beyond 2100.
- Increased coastal protection pressure in **SE Asia, Caribbean/Pacific islands, NYC, Cairo, London**.
- By 2050s: **200 million people** may be permanently displaced (floods, droughts) *(Stern, 2006)*.
- Melting glaciers: initially increase flood risk, then **strongly reduce water supplies** — threatening **1/6 of world population** (Indian subcontinent, China, Andes).

### Ecosystems
- **15–30% of species** potentially facing extinction after only 1.5–2.5°C warming.
- Shifts in geographical ranges of species.
- Harmful consequences for ecosystem goods and services.
- Coral systems cannot handle higher temperatures.
- Wildfires will increase.
- Up to **30% of species** at increased extinction risk.

### Ocean Acidification
- Oceans **30% more acidic** than pre-industrial times.
- Average ocean surface pH ≈ **8.1**.
- Threatens pelagic fish, shell-forming marine creatures, and dependent species.

### Food Security
- **Higher latitudes:** slight crop increase below 3°C, then decrease.
- **Lower latitudes:** productivity may decrease even for small temperature rises.
- Declining crop yields in **Africa and parts of Asia**.
- Hundreds of millions unable to produce/purchase sufficient food.

### Freshwater Supply
- Worsens water-stress from population growth and land use change.
- Severe loss of glaciers and snow fields.
- Changes seasonal flows in Hindu Kush, Himalayas, Andes regions.
- **2 billion people** depend on water from 7 major Himalayan rivers in Asia.

### Water Supply (Continued)
- Changes in precipitation/temperature affect runoff and water availability.
- Semi-arid areas (Mediterranean, southern Africa, NE Brazil) will have **less water**.
- **Drought-affected areas projected to increase**.
- Rising temperatures affect freshwater quality.
- Coastal areas: rising sea levels → **saline groundwater contamination**.

### Public Health
- Health of millions at risk from:
  - Malnutrition, heat stress
  - Diarrheal diseases, vector-borne diseases
  - Heart and breathing problems
  - Climate-induced ground-level ozone
  - Spread of infectious diseases

### Industry, Agriculture, Livelihoods & Society
- Most vulnerable: coastal/river flood plains, climate-sensitive economies, extreme weather-prone areas.
- Agricultural yields affected by water quantity/quality changes.
- Irrigation needs rise in drier areas.
- **Middle East and SE Asia** → increasing water stress.
- CC exacerbates existing stresses: limited water, land degradation, biodiversity loss.

### Migration
- IPCC (1990): Greatest single impact of CC could be on **human migration**.
- Millions displaced by shoreline erosion, coastal flooding, severe drought.
- **Gradual changes** (desertification, drought) affect migration more than extreme events.
- 1979–2008: 718 million affected by storms vs **1.6 billion by droughts** (EM-DAT, 2009).
- Mass migration → overexploitation of resources in destination areas.

### Effects in North America
- Warming in western mountains
- Increased rain helps some crops
- Heat waves increase in number, length, intensity
- Coastal communities: increased flooding and storms

### Ecosystem Effects (Summary)
- Coral systems and unique ecosystems cannot handle higher temperatures
- Wildfires will increase
- Up to 30% of species at increased extinction risk

## 15. Impacts of Climate Change on India

### India's Climate Vulnerability
- **Hottest March on record** in 2022 — 90+ deaths, forest fires, devastated wheat yield.
- By 2100: at least **1/3 of Himalayan glaciers** will be gone *(2019 report)*.
- Bengaluru (2022): torrential rainfall disrupted water supplies.
- Cyclone Yaas (2021): ~150,000 homeless, 25,000 homes destroyed in Eastern India.

### Vulnerable Sectors
- Water resources, coastal ecosystems, biodiversity, agricultural productivity.
- **700 million** in rural areas depend on climate-sensitive sectors.
- Adaptive capacity of dryland farmers, forest dwellers, fisherfolk, nomadic shepherds is very low.

### Water Resources
- Per capita freshwater: **1,900 m³ (2007)** → projected **1,000 m³ by 2025**.
- More intense rain and flash floods → higher runoff, reduced groundwater recharge.
- Himalayan river systems draining into Ganga basin gradually dying out.
- Monsoon changes → severe droughts and intense flooding.

### Agriculture
- **IARI:** Every 1°C rise → India loses **4–5 million tonnes** wheat production.
- Projected warming/rainfall shifts → crop yields may decrease **30% by mid-21st century**.
- **60% of cropland** is rain-fed; poorest farmers most vulnerable.
- 0.5°C winter temp rise → rain-fed wheat yield reduced by **0.45 tonnes/hectare**.
- Major impact on rain-fed crops.

### Health & Disease
- 6 major vector-borne diseases: **Dengue, Malaria, Filariasis, Chikungunya, Japanese Encephalitis, Leishmaniasis**.
- CC enhances transmission season and geographical distribution.
- Malaria transmission likely to widen in northern/western states, shorten in southern states.
- Rising coastal water temps → increased cholera abundance and toxicity.

### Forests & Biodiversity
- **50% of India's forests** likely to experience shift in forest types.
- Adversely impacts biodiversity, regional climate dynamics, forest-based livelihoods.

### Coastal Ecosystems
- **24 of 65 coastal districts** highly cyclone-prone.
- Sea-level rise and temperature increases threaten mega-delta regions.
- Changes in Godavari, Mahanadi, Krishna deltas may displace millions.
- Damage to aquaculture; declining fish productivity.
- **NATCOM (2004):** 1-meter sea-level rise → ~**7.1 million displaced**, **5,764 sq km** land lost, **4,200 km** roads lost.
- Mangroves, coral reefs threatened by temperature, sea level, and CO₂ changes.

### India's Rankings
| Index | India's Position | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Children's Climate Risk Index (CCRI) | 26th of 163 → **8th** (#CCPI) | 2021 / 2023 |
| Environment Performance Index (EPI) | **Last (180th)** of 180 countries, score 18.9 | 2022 |

### India's Commitments
- **NDC (Nationally Determined Contributions)** for 2021–2030 submitted to UNFCCC in 2015.
- Focus: sustainable development, poverty eradication, low carbon path.
- Paris Agreement pledge in 2015.

## 19. How to Prevent Climate Change

1. **Switch to sustainable transportation**
2. **Improve farming** and encourage vegan diets
3. **Restore nature (forestation)** to absorb more carbon
4. **Protect forests** like the Amazon
5. **Protect the oceans**
6. **Reduce plastic**
7. **Global partnership** to obey climate laws

### Individual Actions
- Walk or use environment-friendly transport (bicycles, rickshaws)
- Reduce and recycle waste
- Use energy-efficient appliances
- Plant trees (especially in urban/semi-urban areas)

## 20. India's National Strategies

### NAPCC — National Action Plan on Climate Change

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| **Launched** | 30th June 2008 |
| **By** | Prime Minister's Council on Climate Change |
| **Aim** | Enable India to adapt to CC and enhance ecological sustainability of development |

#### Principles of NAPCC
1. Protect the poor through inclusive, sustainable development sensitive to climate change
2. Achieve national growth and poverty alleviation while ensuring ecological sustainability
3. Extensive deployment of appropriate technologies for adaptation and mitigation
4. New market, regulatory, and voluntary mechanisms for sustainable development
5. Effective implementation through civil society, LGUs, and public-private partnerships
